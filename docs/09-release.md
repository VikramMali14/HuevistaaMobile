# 09 · Release

What it takes to put HueVistaa in Google Play and the App Store. It covers what
only the owner can decide or create, the store listing, the forms, and the order of
the steps. The app's side of each answer below was checked against the code. Store
policies change, so read each form's current wording as you fill it in.

## Before anything: decisions and accounts only the owner can make

| # | What | Why it blocks | Suggested |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How rooms and AI credits are sold in the store builds** | Rooms and AI credits are digital goods used inside the app, and the app sells them through Razorpay in a browser (`/pay/mobile`). Google Play's payments policy requires Google Play Billing for that, or an approved alternative such as user-choice billing in India, which still offers Play Billing beside it. Apple's guideline 3.1.1 requires in-app purchase. Shop codes that unlock rooms may also need explaining to Apple (3.1.1 and 3.1.3). | Android: Play Billing consumables for a room and an AI credit, verified by the backend, which grants them idempotently. iOS v1: no buying in the app. Shop codes and existing balances still work, and the Buy buttons are hidden. Either is real work on both the app and the backend. Until it's done, the payment screens must not ship in a store build. |
| 2 | **Sign in with Apple** | The app offers Google sign-in on every platform (`welcome.tsx`). Apple's guideline 4.8 then expects a privacy-focused equivalent. | Hide Google on iOS for v1; email and phone remain. Add Sign in with Apple later (`expo-apple-authentication`, a backend endpoint that checks Apple's identity token, and revoking it on account deletion). |
| 3 | **Accounts** | Nothing below can start without them. | An Expo account and project (`eas init` writes `extra.eas.projectId`), then `eas update:configure`. A Firebase project for Android push (`google-services.json`, the FCM v1 key uploaded with `eas credentials`). A Sentry organisation (EU region; plan retention of 90 days or less, as the privacy policy says). The Apple Developer Program (Team ID, APNs key). A Google Play Console account. A personal account must run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days before production. |
| 4 | **The package id and first version** | `com.gridstore.huevistaa` can't change after the first Play upload. The version is X5's baseline. | Confirm the id. Ship `1.0.0`: set `version` in app.json, and the backend's `MOBILE_*_MIN_VERSION` stays `0.0.0` until a version must be retired. |
| 5 | **A staging backend** | The Maestro flows make rooms and take boards. Never run them against production. | A separate host and database (for example `staging-api.huevistaa.com`, HTTPS, because release builds refuse plain HTTP), with the accounts and data under "End-to-end tests". |
| 6 | **A native Hindi reader** | `src/i18n/hi.ts` was translated against a glossary and reviewed twice, but not by a native speaker. | One read through the app in Hindi before release, checked against the voice rules (02-design-system.md). |
| 7 | **Product analytics** | docs/01's "measure what matters" needs product events. Crash reports alone don't give them. | Decide on a tool and a consent switch in S1 first. Then the privacy policy and the data-safety form both gain an "app activity" line. Until then, none is collected. |

### App access (for review)

The app signs in with a texted code by default, and reviewers can't receive one. Make
two accounts on production that sign in with **email and a password** ("Use email
instead" on the first screen), and give both in *App access*:

- a **customer** with a ready-made room started, one board and one AI image, and no
  card on file;
- a **painter** with **0 points**. A painter with points could redeem real vouchers.

### Data safety

"Collected" means the data leaves the phone. Everything is encrypted in transit
(HTTPS). Deletion can be requested in the app (Settings, then Delete account) and at
the URL above. No data is sold, and there are no ads. Each processor below works for
HueVistaa (a "service provider"), so none of it is "shared" in Play's sense. Showing a
painter's number to a customer is the painter's own choice. Check this reading against
the form's current wording.

| Data type | Collected | Optional | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name | Yes | No | Account management |
| Email address | Yes | Yes (phone sign-in needs none) | Account management, communications |
| Phone number | Yes | Yes (needed for phone sign-in, and for a painter to be listed) | Account management, app functionality |
| User IDs | Yes | No | Account management |
| Approximate location | Yes: a painter's chosen base, rounded to about 1 km | Yes | App functionality |
| Precise location | Yes: sent with one nearby search, not kept with the account; the web server's access log holds it for about 2 weeks | Yes | App functionality |
| Photos | Yes: the room photographs a customer takes or picks | No (the app's purpose) | App functionality (AI processing to find walls and make images) |
| Purchase history | Yes: rooms and credits bought, on the website's checkout | Yes | App functionality, fraud prevention |
| Other in-app messages | Yes: support chats | Yes | Customer support |
| Other user-generated content | Yes: reviews, questions, and an AI image's note | Yes | App functionality |
| Crash logs, diagnostics | Yes: Sentry, with an internal account id only, kept at most 90 days | No | Analytics (crash diagnosis) |
| Device or other IDs | Yes: the push token, while notifications are on | Yes | App functionality (notifications) |
| App interactions | No (until product analytics is decided — see 7 above) | | |

Payment details are typed into Razorpay's page on the website, not into the app.

What deletion removes and keeps is listed on https://huevistaa.com/legal/delete-account
and matches S9. Rooms, photos, boards, AI images, unused rooms and credits, points,
undelivered vouchers, the nearby listing, support chats and push tokens all go.
Payment and order records are kept for tax.

### Content rating (IARC)

Category: all other app types.
- Violence, fear, sexuality, language, drugs, gambling, crude humour: none.
- Users interact or exchange content: **yes**. Reviews and questions are public and
  moderated before they appear. Support chats are with the team or an AI assistant.
- Shares the user's location with other users: **yes**, opt-in. A painter's rough area
  (about 1 km) is shown to customers searching nearby.
- Shares personal information: a listed painter's mobile number, to a customer who asks.
- Digital purchases: yes, if any are sold in the store build (decision 1).
- Unrestricted web access: no.
- Generated by AI: yes (AI images).

Target audience: 18 and over.

### Permissions

| Permission | Why |
|---|---|
| `CAMERA` | Photographing a room (C6); scanning a board's QR (P3) |
| `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`, `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` (in use only) | Searching nearby (C32); a painter's base (P5) |
| `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` | Walls ready, AI image ready, voucher settled, support reply. Asked only at those moments, after the app says why. |
| `READ/WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` (Android 12 and older) | Saving boards and AI images on older phones |
| `INTERNET`, `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, `ACCESS_WIFI_STATE`, `VIBRATE` | Normal permissions |

Blocked in app.json: the `READ_MEDIA_*` permissions (the photo picker needs none),
background location, the microphone, and `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` (remote push only).
Confirm the merged list on the first AAB: `bundletool dump manifest`.

## App Store

- **Privacy labels**: the same data as Play's table, as "Data linked to you": contact
  info, user content (photos, chats, reviews), location (precise, not stored; coarse,
  for painters), identifiers (user id), purchases, and diagnostics (crash data).
  Tracking: none (`NSPrivacyTracking` false in the app's privacy manifest).
- **Age rating**: 17+ is not needed. Answer user-generated content (moderated) and
  "unrestricted web access: no", and expect 12+ or 4+.
- **Review notes**: the two demo accounts above; a note that shop codes come from
  paint shops at their counters (the shop pays, on the website); and, if decision 1
  goes that way, that nothing is sold in the iOS app.
- **Export compliance**: `usesNonExemptEncryption: false` (HTTPS and hashing only).
- **Universal links**: `applinks:huevistaa.com` is in app.json. The website serves the
  file once `APPLE_TEAM_ID` is set; Apple's CDN can take a day to pick it up.
- **Push**: the APNs key in `eas credentials`.
- **First device pass** before review: the studio (expo-gl), the PDF "Open with",
  Google sign-in (if kept) and payment sessions (if kept), notifications, and Hindi.

## Steps, in order

1. Merge the backend and website branches, then deploy both. The app's X5 check
   fails open, so the order doesn't break anything. The push and deletion endpoints
   must be live before a store build reaches people. Set on the backend:
   `PUSH_ENABLED=true`, `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN`, and `MOBILE_ANDROID_STORE_URL` (and the iOS
   one once it exists).
2. `eas init` and `eas update:configure`. Then add `google-services.json` and the FCM
   key. Then set `EXPO_PUBLIC_SENTRY_DSN`, `SENTRY_ORG`, `SENTRY_PROJECT` and
   `SENTRY_AUTH_TOKEN` as EAS secrets.
3. Decide 1 and 2 above, and build what they need.
4. `eas build -p android --profile production`, then upload to Play internal testing
   with Play App Signing.
5. Put both SHA-256 fingerprints (upload and Play signing) into the website's
   `ANDROID_APP_CERT_SHA256`. App Links then verify.
6. Closed test (12 testers, 14 days for a personal account), with Sentry watching
   crash-free sessions. The listing, data safety, content rating and app access come
   from the sections above.
7. Production. Afterwards, `eas update --channel production` ships JavaScript-only
   fixes for the same native version. A native change needs a new build, and raising
   `MOBILE_ANDROID_MIN_VERSION` retires an old one.
8. iOS: the Apple account, Team ID, APNs key and `APPLE_TEAM_ID` on the website. Then a
   TestFlight build, the first device pass, and review.

## End-to-end tests (Maestro)

`.maestro/` holds three flows, run by `.github/workflows/e2e-android.yml` (manually, and
nightly) on an emulator against staging:

- **customer-board**: a ready-made room, a wall in a shade, a saved combination, then
  the colour board taken.
- **painter-type-code**: a board's code typed in, reaching its claim screen.
- **language**: Settings to Hindi and back.

The staging data they need:
- A customer and a painter that sign in with email and a password, both past About you.
- At least one published ready-made room, and the paint catalogue.
- A board token (`E2E_BOARD_TOKEN`) from a room that a shop's code unlocked, with its
  board handed over.

Secrets live in the repository's `e2e` environment: `E2E_API_ORIGIN`,
`E2E_CUSTOMER_EMAIL`, `E2E_CUSTOMER_PASSWORD`, `E2E_PAINTER_EMAIL`,
`E2E_PAINTER_PASSWORD` and `E2E_BOARD_TOKEN`, plus the variable `E2E_SITE_ORIGIN`.

Not verified here, because this environment has no emulator: the release build under
Expo SDK 57, Maestro reaching each testID, and expo-gl on the emulator's software GPU.
If the canvas doesn't draw there, the board still prints swatches, so the flow passes
without proving the drawing.
